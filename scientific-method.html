🔬 Scientific Method

The scientific method is a systematic process used by scientists to investigate questions, test ideas, collect evidence and reach conclusions based on observations and results.

📚 What You'll Learn

  • Observations

  • Analysing results
  • Drawing conclusions
  • Evaluating experiments

🧪 Steps of the Scientific Method

  1. Observation: Notice something and identify a possible problem or question.
  2. Question: Ask a clear question that can be investigated.
  3. Hypothesis: Make a testable prediction.
  4. Experiment: Design and carry out a fair test.
  5. Collect Data: Record measurements and observations.
  6. Analyse: Look for patterns and relationships in the results.
  7. Conclusion: Decide whether the results support the hypothesis.

💡 Study Tip

Remember that a hypothesis is a prediction that can be tested. When planning an experiment, make sure you identify the independent, dependent and controlled variables.

📝 Example Question 1

Question:

What is a hypothesis?

Answer: A hypothesis is a testable prediction or possible explanation for an observation or question.

📝 Example Question 2

Question:

What is the independent variable in an experiment?

Answer: The independent variable is the factor that the scientist deliberately changes.

📝 Example Question 3

Question:

What is the dependent variable?

Answer: The dependent variable is the factor that is measured or observed during the experiment.

📝 Example Question 4

Question:

Why should an experiment be repeated?

Answer: Repeating an experiment helps make the results more reliable and reduces the effect of random errors.

📝 Example Question 5

Question:

Why are controlled variables important?

Answer: Controlled variables are kept the same so that the investigation is a fair test and the effect of the independent variable can be measured.
